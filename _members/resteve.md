Rebeca Esteve holds a degree in Biotechnology from the University of Valencia, with specialized training in Human Reproduction after completing a Master’s Degree in Biotechnology of Assisted Human Reproduction. She is currently a pre-doctoral researcher at the University of Valencia thanks to a CIACIF grant awarded by the Valencian Regional Government’s Department of Education, Universities, and Employment, co-funded by the European Union (CIACIF/2022/447).  

Specifically, she is currently working on her doctoral dissertation at the School of Medicine (Department of Pediatrics, Obstetrics, and Gynecology) and at the La Fe Health Research Institute—IVI Institute, in the Bioinformatics Department, within the Genomic & Systems Reproductive Medicine group, led by Dr. Patricia Díaz Gimeno.  

Her doctoral thesis focuses on the study of the molecular mechanisms involved in endometrial function and their impact on reproductive outcomes, particularly in the subpopulation of patients undergoing in vitro fertilization who have been diagnosed with benign uterine conditions (e.g., endometriosis, adenomyosis, and fibroids). Specifically, we focus on the transcriptomic analysis of endometrial samples and the identification of biomarkers with potential clinical application in the context of assisted reproduction and/or in the management of patients suffering from these conditions.  

During her previous phase, while working on her master’s thesis, she had the opportunity to conduct research on the embryonic factor by applying artificial intelligence as a noninvasive embryo selection method focused on morphokinetics. While in this new phase she is focusing on the other key factor necessary for achieving pregnancy: the endometrium, thereby gaining a holistic understanding of infertility and its associated factors.  

To date, she is a co-author of a scientific paper published in a high-impact international journal in the field of reproductive biology (Fertility and Sterility), which is based on the results obtained in the aforementioned master’s thesis. In addition, she has presented several scientific papers at leading international conferences, such as those organized by the European Society of Human Reproduction and Embryology (ESHRE) and the American Society of Reproductive Medicine (ASRM).
